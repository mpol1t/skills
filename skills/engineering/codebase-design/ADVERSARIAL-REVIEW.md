# Adversarial review

Use independent challenge when a consequential decision has competing plausible answers, uncertain shared contracts, or failure modes one perspective could miss. The invoking agent remains the **orchestrator**, responsible for the question, evidence, and recommendation. A straightforward decision can stay with one agent.

## Frame and delegate

Name the artifact or decision being examined, settled constraints, open questions, and what evidence could change the answer. Set an effort budget proportionate to the decision before dispatching. A useful starting point is one round of independent assessments followed by one challenge round; extend only while new evidence justifies the cost.

Choose expertise for the actual uncertainty, such as domain behavior, operational failure modes, usability, or dependency analysis. Give agents different obligations to examine rather than asking everyone to endorse the same proposal. Include at least one brief that seeks counterexamples or a simpler alternative. A finding needs a source or concrete failure scenario, its consequence, and the assumption it challenges; no actionable finding is a valid result.

Choose among available models for task capability and, where useful, diversity of approach. Different models can still make correlated mistakes. If model selection is unavailable, use the available model with fresh agents and distinct independent briefs, and disclose the limited model diversity. If delegation itself is unavailable, perform the applicable checks locally and disclose that no independent review occurred.

Control context deliberately:

- **Independent assessment:** use fresh contexts with the question, source pointers, relevant decisions, and constraints, excluding the orchestrator's preferred answer and other agents' conclusions where possible. Reviewing an existing proposal still requires that proposal.
- **Continuation or specialist follow-up:** preserve relevant working context when reconstructing it would lose useful detail. Supply newly discovered evidence explicitly.
- **Challenge round:** share the competing claims and supporting evidence after the independent assessments, then ask agents to address specific objections.

Each brief identifies its scope, source pointers, expected output, model choice where controllable, and context choice. Agents read the necessary sources; freshness is not permission to omit requirements. The orchestrator retains the original assessments so synthesis cannot silently erase dissent.

## Resolve and stop

Compare claims against requirements and sources. Use a focused investigation, test, or prototype when a factual disagreement is decidable that way. Send new evidence back to the relevant agents and revise the artifact or recommendation. Review the consequences of a revision as well as the original objection.

Finish when material objections are resolved with evidence or recorded as explicit tradeoffs within existing authorization. Agent unanimity and majority vote are not acceptance criteria. Preserve substantive dissent, assumptions, and the reason for the chosen recommendation in the current artifact or decision record.

When discussion repeats without new evidence or reaches its effort budget, report the unresolved question and the evidence or decision needed next. Budget exhaustion does not make an artifact ready. Product priorities, scope changes, and unsettled human choices go back to the user; agents never stand in for their answers. Consultation stays within the requested phase and does not authorize publication or implementation.
